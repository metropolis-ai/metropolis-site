# How Metropolis works

This is the public-safe copy source for the operating-model sections of the
future “How it works” page. It follows `docs/positioning.md`: page copy may
compress this argument, but should preserve the distinction between what is
working now and what Metropolis is building toward.

## Hero

**Eyebrow:** The operating model

**Title:** Metropolis is building the studio by using the studio to build
itself.

**Lead:** Metropolis is building a venture studio operated by hierarchical
teams of specialized AI agents. Each agent has a defined responsibility, works
through governed workflow graphs, and reasons from a common knowledge graph
that grows with the institution.

The model is working first in software development. The same structure can
then be abstracted into the other business functions required to plan, build,
and operate companies.

## One system, three connected graphs

A useful AI workforce needs more than capable models. It needs an organization
that assigns responsibility, a process that coordinates work, and institutional
knowledge that keeps each task from beginning at zero.

### A hierarchy of responsibility

Metropolis organizes agents into a hierarchy with explicit responsibilities.
Coordinating agents translate goals into bounded work, delegate that work to
specialists, monitor progress, review results, and escalate when human judgment
is required.

This is not an attempt to recreate job titles in software. A conventional role
is a historical bundle of many kinds of work. Metropolis decomposes those
bundles into narrower capabilities that can be assigned, evaluated, improved,
and recombined.

The hierarchy gives each agent enough context to do its job without asking one
general-purpose agent to understand and operate the entire company at once.

### Workflow graphs that improve through use

Agents do not simply receive a prompt and improvise until they stop. Work moves
through explicit workflow graphs: research, planning, execution, evaluation,
review, and delivery are connected as observable steps with defined inputs,
outputs, and gates.

The graphs improve through operation. Outcomes reveal missing steps. Evaluation
exposes weak work. Founder feedback changes instructions, responsibilities, and
quality bars. Successful methods become reusable capabilities for the next
run.

“Self-improving” does not mean unattended self-modification. Changes remain
visible and governed. The system learns because evidence and feedback are fed
back into how the work is performed.

### A common knowledge vector graph

The agent hierarchy shares a common knowledge graph: research, decisions,
methods, plans, technical context, and lessons from prior work are connected
and retrieved semantically when they are relevant.

That common ground lets specialists share institutional context without
flattening their responsibilities. It also means completed work can improve
more than one future task. The result of a workflow becomes part of what the
institution knows and, when appropriate, part of what its workflows can do.

Access remains bounded. Shared methods and infrastructure can compound across
the studio while sensitive company, customer, financial, and strategic context
stays within the business that owns it.

## The founder leads; the system executes

Metropolis is designed to execute without continuous supervision, but not
without governance.

The founder sets strategy, chooses where to act, allocates attention and
resources, maintains a view of the institution, defines the quality bar, and
remains responsible for consequential decisions. The system translates that
direction into coordinated work and returns results that inform what happens
next.

Two gates remain explicitly human:

1. **Plan approval.** Before consequential work begins, the founder reviews the
   proposed direction, scope, and tradeoffs and either approves it or asks for
   another iteration.
2. **Delivery and decision approval.** Material product changes, pull requests,
   strategic decisions, and sensitive or irreversible actions return to the
   founder for judgment.

The purpose of autonomy is not to remove leadership or responsibility. It is to
concentrate human attention where strategy and judgment have the highest value,
while allowing the work between those decisions to continue.

## How work moves

The operating loop is simple even when the work is not:

1. A business function identifies a question, problem, or opportunity.
2. The system captures it as structured work and places it in context with
   competing priorities.
3. A coordinating agent shapes the work, identifies dependencies, and assigns
   it to the appropriate specialists and workflows.
4. The workflow researches, plans, executes, evaluates, and produces a
   reviewable result.
5. The founder decides at the relevant gate, asking for iteration when needed.
6. The result, evidence, and feedback return to the knowledge and workflow
   graphs, improving what the institution can do next.

The output may be a plan, analysis, decision, design, or working product
change. The underlying loop remains the same: understand, decide, execute,
evaluate, and learn.

## Working from the inside out

Today, the clearest proof of this model is Metropolis's engineering development
team. A hierarchy of agents plans, builds, reviews, and delivers software under
founder governance. The system has produced the infrastructure, planning
surfaces, knowledge tools, and public site that the studio itself uses.

Product and planning are beginning to emerge as a second business function.
Research, finance, design, operations, and other functions remain the direction
of travel, not claims of completed capability.

This sequence is deliberate. Metropolis is not designing an operating model in
the abstract and asking a future company to trust it. The studio is the first
environment in which the model must work. Each new capability is used to build
the institution before it is reused more broadly.

## From studio capability to portfolio capability

As the operating model matures, appropriate parts can be carried into portfolio
companies: agent responsibilities, workflow graphs, evaluation methods,
infrastructure, and shared operating knowledge.

The companies do not become interchangeable. Each keeps its own market,
strategy, customers, products, and private context. What they inherit is a more
capable starting point and a studio that does not need to reconstruct the
machinery of company-building from zero.

That is the recursion at the center of Metropolis: the same kind of system that
helps the studio understand, decide, build, operate, and learn can help each
portfolio company do the same at a narrower scope.

## Closing

**Title:** Build the institution once. Improve it with every company.

**Copy:** Metropolis begins with one working team and expands one governed
capability at a time. The goal is not a company that merely uses AI. It is a
company-building institution whose organization, workflows, and knowledge can
compound through everything it builds.

## Copy guardrails

- Do not expose internal nautical role names. Describe responsibilities and
  operating behavior instead.
- Do not imply that every business function is operating today. Engineering is
  the proof; product and planning are emerging; the rest is the destination.
- Do not describe workflow improvement as uncontrolled self-modification.
- Do not imply that all knowledge is shared without boundaries.
- Do not present agents as synthetic employees or personalities. The unit of
  design is a bounded capability with a responsibility, workflow, and quality
  bar.
- Keep human responsibility explicit. Autonomy changes where the founder
  spends attention; it does not remove approval authority.
